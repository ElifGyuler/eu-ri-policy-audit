01 · The institutional puzzle

How does one EU funding architecture produce very different participation patterns?

The thesis compares Israel and Ukraine because both became associated with Horizon Europe in late 2021 and can participate in the programme under the EU's association framework. The research then asks whether differences in funding patterns can be explained by the rules alone, or whether additional institutional and technological factors are needed.

Russia is used separately as an institutional-capacity benchmark: the European Commission announced on 3 March 2022 that it would suspend cooperation with Russian entities in research, science and innovation, stop new Horizon Europe contracts and suspend payments under existing contracts.

Important timing limitation

The numerical groups in the notebook are 2019–2021 and 2022–2024. Because the second group begins before October 2023, this page describes the result as a broad period comparison rather than a clean causal test of the October 2023 event itself.

05 · Ownership and eligibility

The thesis follows the difference between where an entity is registered and who controls it.

The European Defence Fund regulation sets eligibility and control conditions for recipients, including rules concerning control by non-associated third countries. The thesis cross-references project records with ownership sources to identify Israeli-linked participation through EU-registered entities.

06 · Methods and reproducibility

An explainable computational policy audit.

The research deliberately prioritises auditable project-level processing, interpretable text methods and explicit robustness checks.

01

SEDIA / EU Funding & Tenders project records across the study programmes.

02

Country mapping

Participant-level addresses used to correct country coding before comparison.

03

Statistical audit

Welch's t-test, Mann–Whitney U and Cohen's d for the period comparison.

04

Text analysis

TF-IDF cosine distance plus permutation, placebo and parameter-sensitivity checks.

05

Ownership tracing

Project participants cross-referenced with EDF and corporate-ownership evidence.

Interpretation limits

Titles, not outcomes. The semantic audit analyses project titles; it does not establish actual deployment or end-use.

Small-n period test. The pre/post comparison uses three annual observations per group.

Comparative scope. Similar research-programme status does not make the conflicts themselves equivalent.

Mechanism. Funding patterns and text distances are evidence used by the study; the strategic-autonomy implications remain an interpretation rather than a direct causal estimate.

07 · Policy options proposed in the thesis

Institutional reforms suggested by the author.

These are the thesis author's proposals, separated from the empirical findings above. This page presents them for reference rather than endorsing a political choice.

01

Clarify research-security criteria

Make the criteria for Horizon Europe research-security restrictions more transparent and consistently articulated.

02

Make EDF control guarantees auditable

Increase transparency around Article 9 third-country-control guarantees, ownership and voting control.

03

Parliamentary oversight

Create standing democratic review for security-relevant third-country R&I partnerships.

04

Cross-horizon capability and dependency assessment

Assess both capability gains and dependency risks across research, digital and defence instruments before partnership renewal.
